autism tool agreement.

Revision after Emilio’s methodological review. The two issues he flagged are fixed, effect sizes are reported at every step, and the results are framed as one pattern across three lenses: tools agree more tightly for girls than for boys.

What changed since the last version

  • Fisher FDR family corrected. BH-FDR is now applied only across the relevant 41 within-domain pairs (per cohort), not the full variable combinatorics that had silently included cross-domain pairs we never report. Under the correct family, SSC-preschool RRB (ADI ↔ RBSR) is FDR-significant (p=.003 → p_fdr=.032) — the pair that had washed out before.
  • Resampling made a single draw. The sex-balanced resampling now draws one matched boy subsample per replicate and runs all three lenses on that same sample (draw-once-then-test), instead of each test drawing its own boys. Numbers are unchanged (draws were already seed-aligned); the design is now explicit and robust — exactly the “resample before any test” structure.
  • Effect sizes at every lens (Emilio’s recommendation): Fisher → Cohen’s q & Δr; covariance → ΔCFI; regression → Cohen’s f² & ΔR² — reported alongside significance, and free of it.

03 Sex vs IQ — moderation regression

ADOS ~ tool, with Sex×Tool (M2), IQ×Tool (M3) and both (M4). Female=1, so β>0 = tighter coupling for girls. Effect size = Cohen’s f² / ΔR² for the interaction.

81% of Sex×Tool terms are girls-higher. In the four primary subsamples, FDR-significant: 0 Sex×Tool (M2), 2 surviving IQ control (M4), 7 IQ×Tool (M3). IQ is the dominant moderator; the sex effect emerges mainly after IQ adjustment and in the pooled samples (4 significant M2 across Full/MIND/SSC).

The pattern, across all three lenses Independent of statistical significance, the same signal recurs: more agreement between autism measures for girls than for boys — whether you look at each correlation pair (71% girls-higher), the full covariance matrix (8 of 12 balanced cells differ by sex), or the regressions (81% girls-higher β). The effect at any single pair is small; the signal is in the consistency of direction. One reading: the girls who reach diagnosis show clearer, more concordant symptoms, while camouflaged presentations are under-detected.
