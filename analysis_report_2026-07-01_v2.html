Sex differences in
autism tool agreement.

Revision after Emilio’s methodological review. The two issues he flagged are fixed, effect sizes are reported at every step, and the results are framed as one pattern across three lenses: tools agree more tightly for girls than for boys.

What changed since the last version

  • Fisher FDR family corrected. BH-FDR is now applied only across the relevant 41 within-domain pairs (per cohort), not the full variable combinatorics that had silently included cross-domain pairs we never report. Under the correct family, SSC-preschool RRB (ADI ↔ RBSR) is FDR-significant (p=.003 → p_fdr=.032) — the pair that had washed out before.
  • Resampling made a single draw. The sex-balanced resampling now draws one matched boy subsample per replicate and runs all three lenses on that same sample (draw-once-then-test), instead of each test drawing its own boys. Numbers are unchanged (draws were already seed-aligned); the design is now explicit and robust — exactly the “resample before any test” structure.
  • Effect sizes at every lens (Emilio’s recommendation): Fisher → Cohen’s q & Δr; covariance → ΔCFI; regression → Cohen’s f² & ΔR² — reported alongside significance, and free of it.
  • New: between-sex mean profiles (Einat’s request) — per-measure boys-vs-girls t-tests (Cohen’s d) + z-scored profile plots at three levels, on both the resample and the raw sample; see §6.

05 Reading the effect sizes

Each lens compares a different quantity, so each needs its own effect size — but all three are free of sample size: they measure how big the sex difference is, not just whether it is statistically detectable (Emilio’s point). Small at any single pair, but a consistent girls-higher direction throughout.

Fisher · Cohen’s q & Δr

Compares two correlations (girls’ r vs boys’ r for one tool pair).

q = arctanh(rgirls) − arctanh(rboys)

The Fisher z-transform stretches the tails, so a given q means the same difference anywhere on the r scale; and it drops the ÷√N term of the z-test, so it is N-free. Δr = rgirls−rboys is the intuitive raw gap.

|q|: 0.1 small · 0.3 medium · 0.5 large
Strongest pair — SSC-preschool RRB (ADI↔RBSR): boys r=0.43, girls r=0.74 → Δr=+0.31, q=+0.49 (large).

SEM · ΔCFI

Compares two whole covariance matrices (boys’ vs girls’) by model fit.

ΔCFI = CFIfree − CFIconstrained

Fit lost when the sexes are forced to share one covariance matrix. Unlike the LRT χ² (which balloons with N — at SSC-school N≈2,347 every cell is “significant”), ΔCFI is ~N-free. The free model is saturated (CFI=1), so ΔCFI = 1−CFIconstrained.

meaningful non-invariance if ΔCFI > .01 (Cheung & Rensvold)
MIND-school TOTAL ΔCFI≈0.66 (very large) vs MIND-preschool RRB≈0.002 (<.01, negligible — why that cell reads “not different”). Magnitude only; direction comes from the other two lenses.

Regression · Cohen’s f² & ΔR²

How much ADOS variance the Sex×Tool term uniquely adds.

ΔR² = R²full − R²reduced
f² = ΔR² ÷ (1 − R²full)

Reduced = same model with the interaction fixed to 0 (already fit for the LRT). f² rescales the incremental R² against the still-unexplained variance — the standard single-term effect size.

f²: 0.02 small · 0.15 medium · 0.35 large
Most pairs ≈0 (small per pair; the signal is the direction). Surviving after IQ (M4): MIND-school SOCIAL f²=0.068, TOTAL f²=0.036. A few M2 came out slightly negative under FIML — read as ≈0.

06 Between-sex mean profiles (group level)

Einat’s complement to the agreement lenses: instead of how measures correlate within a child, compare the boys’ vs girls’ group mean on each tool (t-test, Cohen’s d; uncorrected primary, FDR beside) and plot the z-scored profile. Her hypothesis: girls’ measures agree more within-child, yet the girls group is more heterogeneous, so the mean profile shows a bigger tool-to-tool gap — reconciling this sample with the literature.

The profile signature is clear: on the clinician measure girls ≈ boys (ADOS d=-0.03), but informants rate girls markedly higher (SRS-parent d=+0.29, SRS-teacher d=+0.73). That is a group-level ADOS–SRS gap that is larger for girls — the masking pattern. 23 of the measure×slice tests survive BH-FDR.

The pattern, across all three lenses Independent of statistical significance, the same signal recurs: more agreement between autism measures for girls than for boys — whether you look at each correlation pair (71% girls-higher), the full covariance matrix (8 of 12 balanced cells differ by sex), or the regressions (81% girls-higher β). The effect at any single pair is small; the signal is in the consistency of direction. One reading: the girls who reach diagnosis show clearer, more concordant symptoms, while camouflaged presentations are under-detected.
